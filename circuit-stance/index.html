YOUR STRATEGY STARTS BEFORE THE MATCH

PROGRAM YOUR NEXT MOVE.

You take the role of a super AI in a post-apocalyptic world, programming a robot squad to compete for resources. The modular chip system is the centre of the build: combine chip actions to create the behaviours you want to test.

Choose chips for the board +

The current release trailer shows a chip shop beside the hexagonal programming board. Choose modular actions, arrange a build around your combatant and take that setup into battle.

Give the combination a purpose +

Modular actions allow different tactical builds. Treat the arrangement as one plan: decide what you want your robot to do, try a combination that expresses that idea, and use the match to judge whether it works.

Choose a battle stance +

Battle stances add another choice to the build. The release trailer shows stance effects that change cooldown behaviour or which actions can trigger. Pick one to support the plan, then check what happens in the arena.

WATCH YOUR DECISIONS MEET AN OPPONENT

LET YOUR BUILD DO THE TALKING.

Quick, complex turn-based matches put your choices to the test. Preparation and testing connect directly: the chips you choose become a strategy to evaluate against the next arena challenge.

Read the result +

Arena screenshots place the combatants alongside health bars and visible chip boards. Follow those changing battle states and compare the outcome with the plan you built. Did the combination produce the behaviour you were aiming for?

Carry a question into the next build +

A match is feedback for the next experiment. Reconsider a chip choice or the overall combination, then test again. The game's iterative strategy testing is about experimenting, optimizing and validating your tactics through play.

EXPERIMENTATION WITH SOMETHING AT STAKE

RETHINK. RERUN. RISE.

Circuit Stance is a strategy roguelike with a tiered scoring system. Victories move your rank upward; losses bring challenges and the risk of starting over.

One useful testing cycle +

Start with a chip arrangement, take it into a match, observe the result, and revise the build before another test. Compare what happened after the change, rather than assuming a new combination must be better.

Progress through tactical learning +

Ranking gives each encounter weight, while repeated testing helps you understand your build. The next decision can draw on the last battle: which ideas held up, which need another attempt, and what to change for the next opponent.

A world with personality

The game’s flat 2D robot world has plenty of personality. The current release trailer shows a Hall of Fame scene alongside chip programming, battle stances and arena encounters.
